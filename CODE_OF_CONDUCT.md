# Code of conduct

## The standard, adopted rather than reinvented

This project adopts the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/).
Read it there; it is better than a version written from scratch by two people who have never had to
enforce one, and pointing at the canonical text means it cannot quietly drift.

The short of it: be decent, assume competence, criticise work rather than people, and accept that
somebody's first question about this project is not a burden — it is the most valuable thing we get.

## Who enforces it, and what that honestly means

**Two people maintain this project**, and reports reach them at **contact@easybyte.es**.

That is worth stating plainly rather than implying a committee. There is no moderation team, no rotation
and no appeals process. What you get is a reply from a person, usually within a few days, and a decision
that person owns. Reports stay private; say so in the message and it goes no further than the maintainer
who answers.

What we will do about behaviour that violates the Covenant, in order of how much it takes: say so,
delete it, lock the thread, block the account. There is nothing above that available to us.

## The part specific to this project

Hullwork opens pull requests written by a model. Three things follow, and they are conduct rather than
policy:

- **Review the diff, not the author.** A change is wrong or right on its own; that it came from an agent
  is a fact about provenance, not a verdict.
- **Say when a report is machine-generated.** Not a problem — a fact reviewers need, and the same rule
  we hold ourselves to in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
- **Do not paste other people's production data** into an issue to demonstrate something. Error payloads
  carry user data more often than people expect; redact, or describe it instead.
